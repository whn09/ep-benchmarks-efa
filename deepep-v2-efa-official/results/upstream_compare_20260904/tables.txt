EFA vs DeepEP's published CX7-InfiniBand numbers
================================================================

Workload, identical on both sides: 8192 tokens, hidden 7168, top-8,
256 experts, FP8 dispatch, BF16 combine.
Metric, identical on both sides: logical scale-out GB/s INCLUDING the
  local node's share -- upstream says so verbatim, and it is test_ep.py's
  SO column without --ignore-local-traffic.
us = all-rank mean, pooled from every node's log, averaged over reps.
wire% = SO x (N-1)/N / ceiling.  p5en/CX7 ceiling 50 GB/s,
  b300 ceiling 100 GB/s (TWO EFA devices per GPU).
`*` marks a figure whose denominator is OUR assumption, not upstream's
  statement: upstream publishes no NIC line rate.

SM90 HEAD-TO-HEAD -- p5en/H200/EFA vs upstream SM90/CX7, SM MATCHED
  Upstream's EP 8x4 row is SIX SMs, not twelve. Both sides of each row
  below are at the same SM count; see CROSS-CHECK for what that costs.

  upstream row           op             theirs   wire%*          ours    wire% ours/theirs   ours us
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SM90 CX7 EP 8x2, 12sm  dispatch      90 GB/s    90.0%    81.25 GB/s    81.2%      0.903     1502.9 (n=1)
  SM90 CX7 EP 8x2, 12sm  combine       81 GB/s    81.0%    65.50 GB/s    65.5%      0.809     3602.5 (n=1)
  SM90 CX7 EP 8x4, 6sm   dispatch      61 GB/s    91.5%    55.00 GB/s    82.5%      0.902     4030.7 (n=1)

  Read the dispatch rows together: the ratio is the same at both scales,
  so EFA's dispatch deficit against IB is a LEVEL, not a scaling term.
  Combine is the weak op, and it is the one that closes with node count --
  IB is already at 91.5%* wire at 4 nodes and has no room left.

COMBINE IS AMBIGUOUS ON UPSTREAM'S SIDE -- and it matters
  test_ep.py prints `combine` AND `reduced combine`; upstream's table has
  one column. The head-to-head above uses `combine`, the reading that
  FAVOURS us. If upstream's figure is the reduced variant, substitute:

  upstream row                theirs   ours (reduced) ours/theirs
  SM90 CX7 EP 8x2, 12sm      81 GB/s    55.44 GB/s      0.684
  SM90 CX7 EP 8x4, 6sm       61 GB/s    44.94 GB/s      0.737

CROSS-CHECK -- what quoting the WRONG SM count would have been worth
  Upstream's 4-node row is 6 SM. Our 4-node 12-SM cell against it:

    dispatch  12 SM  56.00 GB/s vs 6 SM  55.00 GB/s -> +1.8% free
    combine   12 SM  54.42 GB/s vs 6 SM  52.00 GB/s -> +4.6% free

  Small, because at 4 nodes our dispatch is nearly SM-flat. At 2 nodes the
  same axis is anything but:
    dispatch  12 SM  81.25 GB/s vs 6 SM  53.50 GB/s -> +51.9%
    combine   12 SM  65.50 GB/s vs 6 SM  38.75 GB/s -> +69.0%

  So the axis has to be matched; at 2 nodes it decides the answer.

B300 CONTEXT -- NOT a head-to-head row
  p6-b300 is sm_103, not sm_100, and has TWO EFA devices per GPU
  (100 GB/s vs CX7's 50). So its GB/s cannot be compared to a
  50 GB/s row, and its wire% answers a different question: not
  "how close to the wire" but "can 12 SM fill twice the wire".
  Upstream's only SM100 row is EP 8x2 / 12 SM: 90 GB/s dispatch,
  91 combine -- 2 nodes only, no SM100 4-node row exists upstream.

                      3 combine              3971.7     107.62    80.7%
                      3 reduced combine      3916.4     109.22    81.9%

  Two readings, both true and pulling opposite ways:
    - absolute: b300 2N/24 SM dispatch is 136.56 GB/s against upstream's
      SM100 90, i.e. 1.52x the logical bandwidth -- on 2x the wire.
    - efficiency: that is only 68.3% of b300's own ceiling. 12 SM
      cannot fill 100 GB/s at 2 nodes; the 4-node cell reaches 82.0%
      because per-rank cross-node bytes grow with node count.

  The apples-to-apples SM100 comparison is p6-b200 (ONE EFA per GPU =
  50 GB/s, same ceiling as CX7): 82.6% dispatch / 69.8% combine at
  2N/12 SM, i.e. 0.918 / 0.767 of upstream's SM100 row. Those are AWS's
  numbers, reproduced by us on that cell to 1.6-2.9%; we have no b200
  nodes now, so this repo cannot refresh them.
