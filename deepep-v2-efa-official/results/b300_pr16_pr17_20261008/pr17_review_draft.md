What we see on b300:

1. **Dispatch gets faster, not slower: −2.8% at 8192 tokens.** The reps don't overlap, and the gain
   shows on both nodes (node 1 1063.7 → 1040.2 µs, node 2 1037.2 → 1001.3 µs).
2. **Combine doesn't move: +0.0% at 8192 tokens.** This holds per node too
   (2877.0 → 2876.9 and 2861.2 → 2863.6 µs), so it isn't a gain on one node cancelled by the other.
3. **At 128 tokens everything is within ±0.4%.** With 128 tokens per rank over 12 SM a channel holds
   only a handful of tokens, so a 48-slot chunk never fills and 48 → 16 can't change the order.
   That makes the decode result expected.

So the "local source monopolises the forwarder, combine replays it and idles NVLink/RDMA"
mechanism doesn't show up as a combine gain here. Instead we get a dispatch gain, plausibly from the
same interleaving keeping RDMA busy during dispatch itself.

Could you share the configuration behind the 7-8% / 0.3-0.9% (1.4-2.9% at 6 SM) numbers? In
particular: instance type, `--num-sms`, tokens per rank, hidden/topk/experts, BF16 vs FP8 dispatch,
and `prefer_overlap_with_compute`. If it's p5en or a different SM count, that would explain the
difference. It would also be worth a line in the description saying the gain is
configuration-dependent.

Either way this is a 1-line change with a consistent net gain on the prefill shape and no decode
cost, so +1 from our side.
