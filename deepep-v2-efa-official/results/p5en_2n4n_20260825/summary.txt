HEADLINE
--------
On the packaged EFA installer 1.50.0 stack, `libnccl-net-ofi.so` registers TWO
GIN plugins -- a Libfabric proxy-assisted one (type 2) and Libfabric_GDAKI
(type 5). Both are loaded, and `Loaded gin plugin Libfabric_GDAKI (v14)` prints
either way, but NCCL selects type 2 by default. Two env vars pick GDAKI:

    NCCL_GIN_TYPE=5  NCCL_SYM_GIN_KERNELS_ENABLE=0

    2N / 12 SM / 8192 tok  dispatch  1644.0 -> 1502.9 us  (-8.6%, 74.0 -> 81.2 GB/s SO)
    2N / 12 SM /  128 tok  dispatch   365.1 ->  169.4 us  (2.16x)
    4N / 12 SM / 8192 tok  dispatch  4315.0 -> 3955.3 us  (-8.3%, 51.5 -> 56.0 GB/s SO)
    4N / 12 SM /  128 tok  dispatch  1003.2 ->  184.3 us  (5.44x)

Set them and nothing else. Everything else we tried is neutral or worse, and
the two pending DeepEP PRs stack on top of this for another 33.5% of 2-node
decode dispatch (TABLE 7).

DENOMINATORS  (state these with every rate; a GB/s alone has flipped a
              conclusion before)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
us            per-op mean over ALL ranks -- 16 at 2 nodes, 32 at 4 nodes,
              pooled from every node's log. Node-level means are NOT
              interchangeable with this: combine is layered by node (see
              NODE LAYERING), so a one-node mean of combine can be 14% off.
              Where several reps are pooled, the rep means are averaged.
SO GB/s       scale-out bytes / time, as printed by test_ep.py. test_ep.py:253
              loops over ALL N scale-out destinations including this node's own,
              so WITHOUT --ignore-local-traffic this includes intra-node
              traffic and is NOT a wire rate.
SU GB/s       the printed `bytes` field / time, exactly. That field is
              num_scaleup_bytes (test_ep.py:271) -- all tokens this rank
              received. Scale-out bytes are NOT printed; recover with SO * time.
wire %        SO * (N-1)/N / 50 GB/s.  N=2 -> wire% is numerically equal to SO.
              N=4 -> wire% = SO * 1.5. The (N-1)/N factor assumes the N
              destinations are evenly loaded -- it is an approximation. For an
              exact figure re-run with --ignore-local-traffic (IGNORE_LOCAL=1),
              which makes SO the true cross-node bytes / time.
SO/SU ranges  MIN-MAX ACROSS RANKS at one instant, not run-to-run variance --
              a different statistic from the us column, which is a mean. See
              SPREAD below before concluding anything about variance.
bytes/rank    SCALE-UP (= the printed `bytes`).
              8192 tok: dispatch 395.9-402.4 MB @2N, 441.4-447.7 MB @4N;
              combine 759.7-772.1 MB @2N, 847.0-859.0 MB @4N.

**12 SM is the operating point; 24 SM is a trade this table prices.** At 2 nodes
24 SM gives up 32.8 us of dispatch (+2.2%) to win 875.3 us of reduced combine
(-20.7%), so the layer total drops 14.7%. At 4 nodes the same trade is much
smaller but the same sign: +0.4% dispatch for -2.7% redComb, layer total -1.7%.
It is not free -- the SM budget doubles, and every row here was measured at
--prefer-overlap-with-compute=0, where nothing else wants those SMs.

**6 SM is wrong at every scale.** Against 24 SM, both
metrics stated: at 2 nodes dispatch +49.2% and dispatch+redComb +97.4%; at
4 nodes dispatch only +1.5% but dispatch+redComb +15.8%. Dispatch alone is
nearly flat from 12 to 24 SM (+2.2% at 2N, +0.4% at 4N) -- it is reduced
combine that pays for a small SM count, and that is why the two metrics
disagree so widely at 6 SM.

Four things explain every range printed above, in order of size:

1. For dispatch the range is the DENOMINATOR, not the time. Each rank routes a
   different number of tokens (test_ep.py's get_unbalanced_scores), so its byte
   count varies 1.4-1.6% across ranks, and GB/s = that rank's bytes / that
   rank's time inherits it.
2. INTEGER PRINTING FLOOR. test_ep.py prints GB/s with :.0f. One unit is 1.26%
   of SO at 2N (SO mean 79.6) and 1.79% at 4N (SO mean 55.8), so `79-80` and
   `55-56` are two adjacent integers -- the narrowest range the format can
   express. Nothing sub-1% is visible in these columns at all.
3. Only combine / reduced combine are genuinely spread (16-21% at 2N), and
   there the driver IS time, not bytes. That is the per-node layering below,
   not jitter.
4. REP-TO-REP REPRODUCIBILITY IS 0.02-0.31% on every op including combine
   (right-hand column). The tables' us values are stable to the third digit;
   the SO/SU ranges are a cross-rank snapshot and say nothing about
   reproducibility.


NODE LAYERING -- why every number here is an all-rank mean
----------------------------------------------------------
dispatch is uniform across ranks and across machines. combine and reduced
combine are NOT: they split cleanly by machine.

2N / 24 SM / 8192, pooled over 3 reps, mean over each node's 8 ranks:
                 node1              node2
  dispatch       1535.0             1536.3     (0.1% apart)
  combine        3300.3             3767.7     (13.2% apart)
  redComb        3074.9             3650.3     (17.1% apart)

4N / 24 SM / 8192, pooled over 2 reps:
  dispatch       3991.5  3986.7  3955.9  3956.6   (0.9% spread)
  combine        7862.5  7641.1  7541.1  7798.1   (4.2% spread)
  redComb        7862.8  7647.6  7549.3  7853.4   (4.1% spread)

Which machine is the slow one is fixed WITHIN a round of consecutive reps and
flips BETWEEN rounds -- it is a per-launch property, not per-iteration noise.
All three official 24 SM reps put node2 slow (3764 / 3771 / 3769 us combine vs
3303 / 3299 / 3299); both main 24 SM reps and both pin reps put the other one
slow. The magnitude is stable across all of them at 14-18%. That is why
pooling reps does NOT flatten the 2-node layering, and why the 4-node figure
looks small: at 4 nodes the slow machine differs between the two reps (node4
in rep1 at 8087 us, node1 in rep2 at 8117 us), so pooling averages it away.
Report all ranks, and never a single node's mean of combine.


MECHANISM -- why the pair, and how to confirm which backend ran
--------------------------------------------------------------
NCCL_GIN_TYPE=5 on its own fails at init:

  gin/gin_host.cc:229 (ncclGinValidateSignalRequest)
    NCCL WARN GIN strong signals are required, but the GIN plugin does not
    support them
  gin/gin_host.cc:440 (ncclGinDevCommSetup)
    NCCL WARN GIN: DevComm setup failed on all available backends
  RuntimeError: NCCL exception (/opt/DeepEP/csrc/kernels/backend/nccl.cu:217): 3

The symmetric-memory GIN kernels (NCCL_SYM_GIN_KERNELS_ENABLE, default 1)
require strong signals; GDAKI does not implement them. While type 2 is still a
candidate NCCL silently falls back to it, which is why the default env works
and is also why nobody notices. Excluding type 2 without relaxing the signal
requirement leaves no backend at all. Hence both vars.

Confirming which backend actually ran, with NCCL_DEBUG=INFO:

  type 5 only:   GIN/Plugin: Skipping plugin Libfabric index 3 type 2:
                   NCCL_GIN_TYPE=5 requested
                 devCommCreate: creating 11 contexts / creating 4 contexts
  both arms:     GIN/Plugin: Loaded gin plugin Libfabric_GDAKI (v14)
                 NET/OFI Selected provider is efa, fabric is efa-direct
                 [Proxy Progress] Device N CPU core M    <-- 16 lines in BOTH

So the only reliable discriminator is the "Skipping plugin ... type 2" line.
`Loaded gin plugin Libfabric_GDAKI` prints on the default env too -- it means
the plugin registered, not that it was selected. `[Proxy Progress]` lines are
not a discriminator either; NCCL builds proxy threads for ordinary collectives
regardless of the GIN backend. Both grep extracts are in logs/:
gin_plugin_selection_default.grep.txt and _gin5symgin0.grep.txt.


CONFOUNDS to control before trusting any of this
------------------------------------------------
EP_BUFFER_DEBUG   csrc/elastic/buffer.hpp:1151 builds a stringstream and
                  printfs "CPU side received count" from INSIDE dispatch's host
                  polling loop, i.e. inside the timed region, and the
                  deepep-v2-efa-gdaki-b200 launcher does not forward the
                  variable while run_test_ep.sh does -- so an arm that sets it
                  is not comparable to one that does not. We could not separate
                  its cost from the type-2 arm's own run-to-run spread (dbg off:
                  359.2 / 371.1 us; dbg on: 327.2 / 373.5 us at 2N/12SM/128tok),
                  so no number is claimed. It is off in every table here.
glued log lines   ranks write concurrently to one stdout, so two per-rank
                  records regularly land on one physical line. A parser using
                  re.search per line silently keeps the first and drops the
                  second -- 82 of 91 run tags in logs/ are affected. Use
                  finditer (make_tables.py and parse_ep.py both do) and check
                  the COMPLETENESS AUDIT block for n=16/32 on every op.
one-node means    see NODE LAYERING.
JIT cache         one EP_JIT_CACHE_DIR per arm. GIN device headers get compiled
                  into DeepEP's JIT kernels, so a shared cache serves the other
                  arm's cubin and the A/B shows nothing.
leaked ranks      rc=0 does not mean clean. A previous round's ranks holding
                  GPU memory inflate combine by 2x with nothing in the log to
                  say so. run_test_ep.sh refuses to start on a busy GPU; use a
                  fresh MASTER_PORT per round.
shape args        the pin launcher forwards only --num-processes/--num-sms, so
                  --num-tokens/--hidden/--num-topk/--num-experts must be given
                  explicitly or test_ep.py runs 4096 tok / topk 6 silently.
